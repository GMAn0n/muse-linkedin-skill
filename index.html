LinkedIn Skill for Muse

Post to LinkedIn from Muse — publish posts, read your profile, and track engagement, all through the official LinkedIn API.

Connect your LinkedIn Download the skill

The LinkedIn Skill for Muse is a free, MIT-licensed CLI that lets Meta's Muse AI operate your LinkedIn account: publish text posts, read your profile, and list recent posts through a secure token-based LinkedIn API integration.

Key facts: free and open source (MIT) · setup takes about 5 minutes · no client ID or client secret needed — just your own LinkedIn access token · tokens last ~60 days · every post requires your explicit approval of the exact text.

What you can do

Publish posts

Draft and publish text posts up to 3,000 characters with public or connections-only visibility. Every post is approval-gated: the skill shows you the exact text in chat and only publishes after you say go. A --dry-run mode previews the exact API payload while drafting, so what you approve is exactly what ships.

Read your profile

Pull your LinkedIn identity on demand — name, headline, and LinkedIn ID — so Muse always knows who it's posting as. Read-only and free to run any time.

List recent posts with engagement

Review your recent posts and how they're performing without leaving chat. Note: LinkedIn restricts this to approved partners, so it needs an extra product approval in your developer app — without it the skill reports "not approved" plainly instead of failing silently.

Secure by design

Your access token lives in the platform vault, never in chat or in files. The skill never asks for anyone's client ID or client secret, and every authenticated request goes only to api.linkedin.com.

Connect in 4 steps

  1. Create a LinkedIn developer app

    Go to the LinkedIn Developer Portal and open or create an app. Under Products, enable Share on LinkedIn (lets the skill publish posts) and Sign In with LinkedIn using OpenID Connect (lets the skill read your profile).

  2. Mint an access token

    Use the portal's token generator to mint an access token with those scopes and copy it. That's the only credential the skill ever needs.

  3. Install the skill files
    cp -r linkedin ~/workspace/skills/

    Get the linkedin/ folder from the GitHub repo or the download page.

  4. Connect it in Muse

    In chat, ask Muse: "set up a LinkedIn connector." Muse gives you a secure form — paste your access token there. It goes straight to the platform vault; never paste it in chat. Then try: "read my LinkedIn profile."

    python3 ~/workspace/skills/linkedin/bin/linkedin.py profile

Frequently asked questions

How do I connect my LinkedIn account to Muse?
Create an app in the LinkedIn Developer Portal, enable the Share on LinkedIn and Sign In with OpenID Connect products, mint an access token with the portal's token generator, copy the linkedin/ skill folder to ~/workspace/skills/linkedin/, then ask Muse to "set up a LinkedIn connector" and paste your token into the secure form. Setup takes about 5 minutes.
What can the LinkedIn skill for Muse do?
Three things: read your LinkedIn profile (name, headline, LinkedIn ID), publish text posts up to 3000 characters with public or connections-only visibility, and list your recent posts with engagement — all through the official LinkedIn API.
Does posting require my approval?
Always. Every post needs your explicit approval of the exact text in chat before anything publishes. The skill drafts the text, shows it to you verbatim, and only posts after you say go. A dry-run mode previews the exact API payload during drafting.
How long does the LinkedIn access token last?
LinkedIn access tokens last about 60 days. When one expires, re-mint it in the Developer Portal and reconnect — the skill tells you exactly this if a call fails with an authentication error.
Why can't the skill list my recent posts?
Listing your own posts back requires LinkedIn's partner-restricted scope, which LinkedIn only grants to approved partners. Request it in your app's Products tab; without it, the posts command reports "not approved" instead of failing silently.
Is the LinkedIn skill for Muse free?
Yes. The skill is free and MIT-licensed, and the LinkedIn API scopes it uses (profile read, posting) carry no usage fees. You just bring your own free LinkedIn developer app and access token.
